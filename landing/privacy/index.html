What HidraNet knows about you

As little as possible, by design. This policy says exactly what reaches anyone, who they are, and what you can do about it. It covers the GDPR and Brazil's LGPD.

2. The principle

HidraNet is designed to know as little as possible about the people who use it. There are no mandatory accounts, addresses are generated on your computer, and chat and mail content is encrypted before it leaves your device.

What HidraNet never stores and never logs:

  • your browsing or search history;
  • the content of your HidraChat conversations or HidraMail messages;
  • your private keys;
  • your IP address together with your browsing activity;
  • browser telemetry or usage reports.

One exception is processing while you are connected, not storage: if you use HidraVPN, its nodes are run by the HidraNet maintainers during the preview. In one-hop mode the node sees your IP address and your traffic at the same time, and in two-hop mode the maintainers run both the entry, which sees your address, and the exit, which sees your traffic (section 4.10). The nodes write neither your address nor your destinations down.

Since we do not store this data, we cannot hand over records of it, nor lose it in a breach. Someone who compelled or compromised a HidraVPN node could still watch what it carries while you are connected.

3. Where your data lives

Most of the data the software uses stays only on your computer: preferences, your address keys, received mail, sites you created and Tor state. Keys are sealed with your operating system's keychain where one is available. HidraNet has no access to any of it. You can delete it at any time by deleting the browser's data folder.

4.3 Search

HidraSearch sends queries through Tor to third-party search engines (DuckDuckGo, SearXNG, Brave Search). The engine sees the search text and the Tor exit address. The browser keeps no search history.

4.4 Network count (optional, off by default)

If you turn on "Count me on the network" in the browser's Anonymity Settings, the browser sends a presence signal every 30 seconds, made of a random code that changes each session, the software version and the time. It carries no location, no room, no name and no address. The signal goes to a public channel and is used only to count how many browsers are running.

Legal basis: your consent (GDPR art. 6(1)(a); LGPD art. 7, I). The option is off by default, and turning it off stops the signal immediately.

4.5 This website

When you visit this website, the hosting provider (Vercel Inc.) logs technical request data, including the IP address, to serve the pages and protect them against abuse. Legal basis: legitimate interest in keeping the site available and secure (GDPR art. 6(1)(f)).

The website uses no analytics, no advertising, no cookies and no third-party scripts, and its fonts are served from our own server.

The review page (hidranet.org/r/, also installable as a phone app) connects to third parties. To show and publish reviews, it connects your browser directly, without Tor, to the public MQTT brokers of EMQ Technologies (EMQX, broker.emqx.io) and, if that one does not answer, HiveMQ (broker.hivemq.com). They see your IP address and which business you look at. A review you post, with its stars, its text, the name and the place you enter, and the time, is published for anyone to read and retained on those brokers. The page then sends the business owner an encrypted HidraMail notice through the same broker. HidraNet does not control the operators' retention policies. The page says this next to the review form. In the HidraNet Browser, HidraReview reaches the same brokers through Tor (section 4.2).

4.6 Paid services

When paid plans or business services are offered, we will process the data needed for the contract and invoicing: name or company name, tax number, billing address, email and payment history. Card data will be handled by the payment provider and never reach HidraNet. Legal basis: performance of the contract and tax obligations (GDPR art. 6(1)(b) and (c)). Retention: ten years for invoicing records, as Portuguese tax law requires; other data up to two years after the contract ends. HidraVPN passes, sold for HNET during the preview, are covered in section 4.10: buying them asks for none of this data.

4.7 HNET and the support wallet

Support sent in HNET goes to a public Solana wallet. Solana is a public ledger: the sending wallet, the amount and the time are visible to anyone, permanently, and HidraNet cannot change or delete them. We do not ask who is behind a wallet and do not try to find out.

4.8 Contact and support requests

If you write to us, we process your address and the message to reply. Legal basis: legitimate interest in answering people who contact us. Retention: two years after the last exchange.

4.9 Security reports

Vulnerabilities may be reported anonymously. If you identify yourself, we keep your name to credit you, and only with your permission.

4.10 HidraVPN (preview)

HidraVPN is a separate program, installed from the browser's VPN page, that carries all of this computer's traffic over WireGuard through one node (one hop) or through an entry and an exit (two hops). During the preview, every node on the official node list and the pass issuer at passes.hidranet.org are run by the HidraNet maintainers. Unlike the browser's Tor routing, this means HidraNet itself processes your traffic while you are connected.

WhoWhat it sees while you are connectedWhat it writes down
The node, in one-hop modeYour IP address and your traffic at the same time: the sites and services you reach, the names you look up, and any content not encrypted end to endA key made for that connection (or, with no pass left, the last pass's key), the tunnel address it was given, and when it was admitted and last seen; deleted six hours after the last handshake

The exit, in two-hop modeYour traffic, as above, coming from the entry's address, not yoursThe same kind of record; deleted six hours after the last handshake
The pass issuerYour account token, over HTTPS, and the address a request comes from, when your computer fetches or buys passesSee below

Two hops keep your address and your traffic on different machines, but until independent operators run entries, not with different people. Two hops under one operator are refused unless you allow it, and the VPN page says so.

The names you look up go through the tunnel to the exit, which asks DNS-over-HTTPS resolvers in turn. The node software's defaults are Quad9 (dns.quad9.net) and Cloudflare (cloudflare-dns.com). They see the names and the exit's address, not yours.

Passes are signed blind: the issuer does not learn which node a pass is for, and a node does not learn which account a pass came from. A node keeps a hash of each pass it has taken, so that the pass cannot be spent twice, until the issuer key it was made under expires. The automatic top-up asks the issuer only while HidraVPN is connected, through the tunnel, so the issuer then sees an exit's address. Fetching passes by hand, or buying them, while HidraVPN is not connected shows the issuer your own IP address.

The issuer keeps two files. The accounts file holds, for each account, a hash of its token (never the token), a name (random for an account made by a purchase; chosen by the maintainers for an account they hand out, and they know whom they gave it to) and the balance or daily quota with the day's use, for as long as the account exists. The orders file holds, for each purchase, the token's hash, the pack, the exact amount, the time and, once paid, a hash of the paying transaction's signature. An unpaid order is no longer needed after 24 hours, a paid order and the hash of the transaction that paid it after about two days. The issuer deletes them from the file the next time anyone opens or claims an order, so on a quiet issuer they can stay longer. While an order is kept, its exact amount and time can be matched against Solana's public ledger to find the wallet that paid (section 4.7). Pay from a wallet you use for nothing else.

No log of the nodes or the issuer records your IP address, the names you look up, an account token, a pass, a wallet, a transaction or an amount. Their system journals do record when things happen: a node notes when it admits a client and when idle clients expire, the issuer when it issues passes and when an order is opened or claimed, with the number of passes and with no address, token or wallet. How long the journals keep these lines: to be confirmed. The issuer is served through a TLS proxy on the same machine; whether that proxy keeps access logs: to be confirmed.

When you run the leak test on the VPN page, it asks api.ipify.org (and api6.ipify.org) which address it sees, only while HidraVPN is connected, so the service sees the exit's address. The browser downloads the node list and the release manifest from this website, and the VPN's programs from GitHub, through Tor, so neither sees your address.

Legal basis: performance of the contract, to carry your traffic and to issue, sell and redeem passes (GDPR art. 6(1)(b)), and legitimate interest in limiting abuse, for the leases, the hashes of spent passes and the rate limits (GDPR art. 6(1)(f)) to be confirmed.

7. Transfers outside the European Economic Area

Some of the third parties above may process data outside the EEA or Brazil, including in the United States. When HidraNet engages a processor in that situation, it relies on a European Commission adequacy decision or standard contractual clauses (GDPR art. 46; LGPD art. 33).

8. Your rights

Under the GDPR (arts. 15 to 22) and the LGPD (art. 18), you have the right to:

  • know whether we process your data and obtain a copy;
  • correct inaccurate data;
  • request erasure;
  • restrict or object to processing;
  • receive your data in a portable format;
  • withdraw consent, without affecting earlier processing;
  • not be subject to solely automated decisions. HidraNet makes none.

Since most data lives only on your computer, many of these rights are exercised directly: delete the data folder. For data we do process (sections 4.5 to 4.10), write to privacy@hidranet.org. We reply within one month, extendable under GDPR art. 12, or within fifteen days for data subjects covered by the LGPD.

One limit follows from the design: we cannot identify or delete messages retained on third-party brokers, forum posts or encrypted content, because we do not know whom they belong to. Forum posts can be hidden by moderation.

9. Supervisory authorities

You may complain to the Portuguese data protection authority (CNPD, www.cnpd.pt), to the data protection authority of your EU country or, in Brazil, to the National Data Protection Authority (ANPD, www.gov.br/anpd).

10. Minimum age

HidraNet is intended for people aged 16 or over. We do not knowingly process data of younger people.

11. Security

We apply client-side encryption, session isolation, data minimisation and an incident response process, with notification to the authority within 72 hours where the law requires it.

12. Changes

Each version of this policy is published with a date. If a change reduces your rights or widens processing, we announce it beforehand on this website and in the browser release notes.
