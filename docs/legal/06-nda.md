### Clause 1 — Confidential Information

1. **Confidential Information** means all information, of any nature or medium,
disclosed by one Party (the "Disclosing Party") to the other (the "Receiving
Party") in connection with the Purpose, in particular:

   a) Unpublished source code, architecture, technical specifications and
      engineering documentation;
   b) Known security vulnerabilities and their remediation plans;
   c) Financial information, projections, cap table and investment terms;
   d) Commercial strategy, pricing, customer and partner lists;
   e) Unpublished product plans and development roadmaps;
   f) Personal data to which access is given;
   g) The existence and the content of the discussions between the Parties.

2. Information is confidential whether or not it is marked as such, wherever by
its nature or by the circumstances of disclosure it should be understood to be
restricted.

### Clause 2 — Exclusions

Information is not Confidential Information where the Receiving Party
demonstrates that it:

   a) Is or becomes public without breach of this Agreement;
   b) Was already lawfully in its possession before disclosure, with no duty of
      confidence;
   c) Was lawfully obtained from a third party under no duty of confidence;
   d) Was independently developed without reference to the Confidential
      Information, which it must prove by documentation.

**Clarification as to free software:** the HidraNet software published under the
MIT licence is not Confidential Information. This Agreement does not restrict use
of the published code, which is governed by that licence.

### Clause 3 — Obligations

The Receiving Party undertakes to:

   a) Keep the Confidential Information confidential, with the degree of care it
      applies to its own restricted information and never less than that of a
      prudent manager;
   b) Use it exclusively for the Purpose;
   c) Not disclose it to third parties without prior written consent;
   d) Limit access to its directors, employees and advisers who need it for the
      Purpose, and only once they are bound by an equivalent duty of confidence,
      the Receiving Party remaining liable for their breach;
   e) Not copy or reproduce it, save to the extent necessary for the Purpose;
   f) Not reverse-engineer components not published under a free licence;
   g) Notify the Disclosing Party without delay of any unauthorised disclosure or
      access of which it becomes aware.

### Clause 4 — Disclosure required by law

1. If the Receiving Party is legally required to disclose Confidential
Information, it shall, **to the extent the law permits**:

   a) Notify the Disclosing Party in advance, as early as possible, so as to
      allow it to react;
   b) Disclose only what is strictly required;
   c) Use its efforts to ensure the disclosed information is treated as
      restricted.

2. The Parties acknowledge that certain legal orders may prohibit the
notification in paragraph (a), in which case it is not required.

### Clause 5 — Security vulnerabilities

1. Given the nature of the project, the Parties acknowledge specific treatment
for security vulnerabilities.

2. On becoming aware of an unpublished vulnerability, the Receiving Party
undertakes to:

   a) Report it to **security@hidranet.org**;
   b) Not disclose it publicly for **90 days** or until it is fixed, whichever
      occurs first;
   c) Not exploit it, nor allow third parties to exploit it.

3. Once the period in the preceding paragraph has elapsed without a fix, the
Receiving Party may proceed with coordinated disclosure, a standard and
legitimate practice in security research. **This right cannot be excluded by the
other clauses of this Agreement.**

### Clause 6 — Ownership

This Agreement transfers no intellectual property rights. The Confidential
Information remains the property of the Disclosing Party.

### Clause 7 — No obligation to contract

This Agreement does not oblige either Party to enter into any transaction, nor
does it confer exclusivity.

### Clause 8 — Term

1. This Agreement runs for **[2] years** from the date of signature.

2. The confidentiality obligations continue for **[3] years** after the Agreement
ends or after the last disclosure, whichever is later.

3. Where the information constitutes trade secrets within the meaning of the
applicable law, the obligations continue for as long as it retains that
character.

### Clause 9 — Return and deletion

On termination of the Agreement or at the Disclosing Party's request, the
Receiving Party returns or deletes the Confidential Information within **30
days**, confirming this in writing, save for:

   a) Copies required by law or by an archiving policy, which remain subject to
      this Agreement;
   b) Copies in automated backup systems, not accessible in ordinary use, to be
      deleted in the normal retention cycle.

### Clause 10 — Personal data

Where there is access to personal data, the Parties comply with the GDPR and,
where applicable, enter into a processor agreement under Article 28.

### Clause 11 — Breach

1. The Parties acknowledge that breach may cause harm not adequately remediable
by damages, and the injured Party may seek injunctive relief.

2. The right to compensation for the loss actually suffered is preserved.

### Clause 12 — Final provisions

1. **Amendments** require written form and the signature of both Parties.

2. **Partial invalidity:** the invalidity of one clause does not affect the
others, and it shall be replaced by one that best matches the Parties' intent.

3. **Assignment:** neither Party may assign its position without the other's
written agreement, save in favour of a company in the same group or in the event
of a corporate restructuring, on notice.

4. **Governing law:** Portuguese law.

5. **Jurisdiction:** the district of Lisbon, with express waiver of any other.

## Annex — Usage guide (not part of the Agreement)

**When to use it:**

| Situation | Use an NDA? |
|---|---|
| First conversation with an investor | Generally **no** — most VCs refuse to sign an NDA in a first conversation, and insisting signals inexperience. Simply do not reveal what you cannot reveal. |
| In-depth due diligence | **Yes** |
| Commercial partner or reseller | **Yes** |
| Service provider with access to systems | **Yes**, with an IP assignment clause |
| Job candidate in a technical interview | **Yes** |
| Security researcher | **No** — use a responsible disclosure policy; an NDA that blocks coordinated disclosure drives away the people who help us |

**Typical terms:** 1–3 years of duration, 2–5 years of confidentiality. Very long
terms are frequently refused and make signature harder.
