# 06 — Go-to-Market

## 1. The sequence, in three phases

They are not three parallel phases. They are sequential, and phase 2 does not
start without phase 1's numbers.

---

## Phase 0 — Before selling anything at all *(month 0–2)*

**Goal: have something to sell and know what selling costs.**

1. **Submit to NGI Zero.** Deadlines every two months, light application,
   €5–50k, no dilution, and the project is already MIT. There is no reason for it
   not to be submitted. See [13](13-funds-and-applications.md).
2. **Close the kill switch gap**
   ([03, section 1.4](03-product-and-technology.md)). You do not sell a privacy
   browser with a known IP leak window.
3. **Billable HidraReview:** multi-tenant, sign-up, payment (see the note on
   processors in [10](10-legal-and-regulatory-risk.md)), owner panel.
4. **10 pilot businesses, free, in person.** Not for revenue — to find the real
   CAC and churn, which is what decides the entire model.

**Gate to pass:** 10 pilots using it weekly + CAC measured.

---

## Phase 1 — Proof of sale *(month 3–9)*

**Goal: 100 paying businesses within a tight geographic radius.**

**Main channel: direct, in-person selling, in a single city.**
Porto or Lisbon. One city, one neighbourhood at a time. Do not scale geography
before the model works on one block.

The sales argument, in 30 seconds:
> "How many reviews did you get this month? And how many customers did you serve?
> The difference is what we are losing. This card on the table converts at 60 to
> 80% — a QR code converts at 35 to 50%. Try it for a month, free."

**Secondary channels:**
- **Trade and restaurant associations** — sell to a group at a time instead of
  door to door.
- **Referral with an incentive** — one free month per business referred. Local
  commerce runs on peer trust; it is the cheapest channel.
- **Resellers:** local marketing agencies and accountants already sell to this
  customer and already have their trust. 30% margin on the recurring fee.

**Metrics to follow (in this order of importance):**
1. **Monthly churn** — kills or saves the model
2. CAC per channel
3. Reviews generated per business (this is the proof of value)
4. Free-to-Pro conversion

**Gate to pass:** 100 paying, churn <5%, CAC <€80, LTV:CAC >3:1.

---

## Phase 2 — Scale *(month 10–24)*

Only starts once phase 1's gates are met. If churn is 8%, **do not scale — fix
the product.** Scaling a model that does not close is the classic way to burn a
seed round.

- Replicate city by city with the phase 1 playbook.
- Brazil: a far larger market, same language. See
  [11](11-jurisdiction-comparison.md) on the structure.
- Hiring: 1 salesperson per ~150 active customers.
- Digital marketing only now: with a known CAC, there is something to compare
  against.

---

## 2. The browser: distribution, not sales

The browser is not sold. It exists to:

- Provide **technical credibility** in fund applications and with investors.
- Drive **organic acquisition** in privacy communities (r/privacy, Hacker News,
  Privacy Guides, Portuguese-speaking technology communities).
- Act as a **top-of-funnel product**: whoever installs the browser discovers
  HidraReview.
- **Every HidraReview business is a node** — that is the bridge between the two.

**Browser launch sequence:**
1. A security audit **before** any broad public release. Launching a privacy
   browser and being publicly taken apart by a researcher is an event you do not
   recover from. NGI Zero offers audits to grantees — one more reason to apply
   first.
2. Portuguese-speaking technical communities first (a more forgiving audience,
   better feedback).
3. Only then, international reach.

---

## 3. Positioning — the exact words

**For the business (who pays):**
> "More reviews, with one tap. Your page is yours, not Google's."

**For the browser user:**
> "Privacy you can verify. If we cannot confirm you are anonymous, we tell you."

**For the investor:**
> "We sell reputation to local business. Every customer becomes a node on a
> post-quantum privacy network that no review competitor can copy."

**For the public-funding evaluator:**
> "Next generation internet infrastructure: onion routing with quantum-resistant
> cryptography, open source under MIT, a European common good."

Four audiences, four sentences. **The same company, different angles** — not four
different stories. If they do not fit together when challenged, the problem is
the strategy, not the communication.
