## 2. Division of roles — recommendation

With two founders and two products, the wrong split is by product (one takes the
network, the other HidraReview): that creates two companies instead of one.
**Split by function.**

| Function | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **CEO** | Revenue, investors, applications, legal, hiring. The one who sells. |
| **CTO** | Product, engineering, architecture, security, audits. |

**Both sell in the first year.** With 0 customers there is no room for a founder
who does not talk to the market — and a CTO who has never heard a customer builds
the wrong thing.

**Who is who:** your decision. A useful criterion: whoever has more appetite for
uncomfortable conversations (sales, investors, lawyers) should be CEO. It is not
whoever "understands the product best".

## 3. Cap table — recommendation

### The mistake to avoid: 50/50

50/50 looks fair and is the structure that kills the most companies. In a
disagreement there is no tie-breaking mechanism, and the company locks up at
exactly the moment it needs to decide quickly.

**Recommendation:** an asymmetric split, even if only slightly (**51/49** or
**55/45**), agreed now, while agreeing it still costs nothing. Criteria: who had
the idea, who has put in more time so far, who takes the CEO role, who goes
full-time first.

### Proposed cap table (at incorporation)

| Shareholder | % |
|---|---|
| Founder A (CEO) | 52% |
| Founder B (CTO) | 48% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

*Employee pool (ESOP) created at the pre-seed round, typically 10%, diluting both
proportionally.*

### Expected evolution

| Phase | Founders | ESOP | Investors |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incorporation | 100% | — | — |
| Pre-seed (€300–500k) | ~76% | 10% | ~14% |
| Seed (€1.5–3M) | ~60% | 12% | ~28% |

Keeping the founders with a comfortable majority after the seed is the goal. A
pre-seed round that dilutes more than 20% is a bad sign for the rounds that
follow.

## 4. Vesting — not negotiable

**Both founders on vesting, from incorporation.**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Period | 4 years |
| Cliff | 1 year (nothing vests before 12 months) |
| Vesting | Monthly after the cliff |
| Acceleration | Single trigger on acquisition: 50% |

**Why, even between friends:** if one of the two leaves after 8 months, with no
vesting they take half the company and the other person works for years for an
absent partner. No serious investor enters a company without founder vesting —
they will require it at the round, and by then the negotiation is far harder than
it is today, while the company is still worth nothing.

**It is the most uncomfortable and most important conversation the two of you
will have. Have it now.**

## 5. Governance

**Early phase (pre-investment):**
- Management: both founders
- Day-to-day decisions: whoever holds the function
- Structural decisions (a round, a senior hire, a change of strategy, a sale):
  both must agree
- Fixed weekly meeting, with written minutes — even if short

**After pre-seed:** an advisory board or a board with 2 founders + 1 investor.

## 6. Documents to sign at incorporation

| # | Document | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Articles of association** with vesting and pre-emption clauses | The legal basis of everything |
| 2 | **Shareholders' agreement between founders** | Vesting, exit, tie-breaking, non-compete |
| 3 | **Intellectual property assignment** | All the code written so far belongs to the founders personally. **It has to pass to the company.** An investor will check this. |
| 4 | Employment or service contracts | Tax treatment |
| 5 | Mutual confidentiality agreement | |

**Document 3 is the most forgotten and the most dangerous.** Without an IP
assignment, the company does not own the product it says it sells. It is an
absolute blocker in due diligence.

## 7. Team gaps to acknowledge

| Gap | When it is resolved | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales** | Q2–Q3 | Founders sell first; hire at the 100th customer |
| **Legal/compliance** | Q1 | External; do not hire |
| **Offensive security** | Q2 | External audit (NGI Zero) |
| **Finance** | Incorporation | External certified accountant |

**In an investor conversation, acknowledging the gaps buys credibility.** Saying
"there are two of us and we know we are missing sales, here is the plan" is
stronger than presenting a fictional team of advisors.
