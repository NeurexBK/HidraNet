# 02 — Market Analysis

**Research date:** September 2026. Every number is sourced at the end of the
document. Where sources disagree, the range is given instead of a single number —
a TAM presented with false precision is the fastest way to lose credibility in an
investment meeting.

---

## 1. The privacy market in 2026

### 1.1 Size

The closest proxy for HidraNet's addressable market is the VPN market, because
that is where the willingness to pay for network anonymity sits:

| Source | VPN market 2026 | CAGR |
|---|---|---|
| The Business Research Company | $86.02bn (from $71.25bn in 2025) | 20.7% |
| Precedence / IMARC | $83.16bn | — |
| Other estimates | $108.57bn | — |

**Honest range: $83–108bn in 2026**, projected at $182bn in 2030 (CAGR ~20.6%).
The spread between sources (~30%) reflects different market definitions — some
include enterprise VPN, others only consumer.

> **How to present this to a VC:** never say "our market is worth a hundred
> billion". Say "the consumer VPN market, our closest proxy for willingness to
> pay, sits between $83bn and $108bn and grows at about 20% a year; our real SAM
> is in section 1.4".

### 1.2 Venture funding in the sector

The capital exists and is growing:

- Cybersecurity and privacy raised **$10.6bn** in 2026 (all stages).
- In **Q2 2026** alone: $4.4bn across seed to growth.
- 2025 closed at **~$14bn across 392 rounds** — up **47%** on the $9.5bn of 2024.
- In Europe, pre-seed typically sits at **€500k–3M** and seed at **€1–5M**.

**Reading:** capital is available, but the bar rose with it. In 2026 a European
€1–5M seed in privacy demands demonstrable traction, not a demo.

### 1.3 Structural demand: the regulatory context is our tailwind

**Chat Control** (the EU CSAR regulation, which would require client-side
scanning before encryption, in practice bypassing E2E) was **rejected on 26 March
2026 by one vote**, and sent back to the EU Council. It is neither dead nor in
force.

This matters to HidraNet in two opposite ways, and both have to be in the deck:

- **In favour:** every Chat Control cycle produces a measurable spike in privacy
  tool adoption. It is demand generated by the legislator itself.
- **Against:** if it passes in a form that covers "number-independent
  interpersonal communication services", HidraChat and HidraMail are potentially
  in scope. See [document 10](10-legal-and-regulatory-risk.md).

One further note: there are already signs of a risk of *de facto* bans for
no-logs providers with a significant EU customer base.

### 1.4 TAM / SAM / SOM

Built bottom-up, not top-down. Every line has to be defensible.

**TAM — Total Addressable Market**
Global consumer privacy and local reputation market where HidraNet has a
product: $83–108bn (VPN/privacy) plus the local reputation and reviews market. A
vanity number; it only frames the space.

**SAM — Serviceable Addressable Market**
What we can serve with the products that exist today, in Portugal, Brazil and
Portuguese-speaking EU:
- ~2–2.5 million daily Tor network users globally — our natural initial audience
  for an anonymity network.
- Micro-businesses and local establishments in PT + BR with a review presence:
  this is where the *paying* SAM is. See 2.2.

**SOM — Serviceable Obtainable Market (36 months)**
Modelled in [document 07](07-financial-projections.md): hundreds to low
thousands of paying businesses on HidraReview plus tens of thousands of browser
users. A small, credible SOM is worth more in a meeting than a large, invented
one.

## 2. HidraNet's two markets

This is the central analysis of the package. HidraNet operates today in two
markets with opposite economics.

### 2.1 Market A — Anonymity network and privacy browser

**Size:** enormous. **Willingness to pay: close to zero.**

Real Tor network figures in 2026:
- **2.0–2.5 million** direct users per day (some sources suggest ~3M; reported
  peaks run well above that).
- **7,500–9,500** active relays, of which only **1,500–2,000 are exit nodes**.
- **~800,000** active v3 onion services per day.

The most important figure for us: **the Tor network has plateaued**. Daily users
in the low millions for years, onion service counts stable. This is not an
exploding market — it is mature infrastructure. That cuts the easy narrative of
"Tor is growing and we ride the wave".

**The economic problem with this market:** the direct competitors are free. Tor
Browser: free, non-profit. Mullvad Browser: free, with no advertising business at
all. Brave: free. There is no market for a "paid privacy browser" because the
reference price is zero.

**Conclusion:** Market A is where the mission, the differentiating technology and
the eligibility for public funding are. **It is not where short-term revenue is.**

### 2.2 Market B — HidraReview: reputation and reviews for local business

**Size:** much smaller. **Willingness to pay: proven.**

- Review NFC card market: **$31.38M in 2026 → $90.01M in 2035**. Small, but it is
  hardware — the software layer on top is what has value.
- **NFC conversion rate: 60–80%**, against 35–50% for a QR code. This is the
  sales metric: a business goes from ~40% to ~70% conversion on review requests.
- Reviews are one of the main ranking signals in local search. A local business
  has revenue tied directly to this.

**Why this is the market that funds the other:** a restaurant owner pays €19/month
for more reviews because they can connect it to turnover. A privacy browser user
does not pay €19/month for anonymity that Tor gives them for free.

### 2.3 The strategic tension — and the answer

| | Market A (Network) | Market B (HidraReview) |
|---|---|---|
| TAM | $83–108bn | tens of $M (hardware) + SaaS |
| Customer | Individual consumer | Local business (B2B) |
| Willingness to pay | ~0 | €9–29/month, proven |
| Competition | Tor, Brave, Mullvad — free, years ahead | Fragmented, many small players |
| Sales cycle | Viral / none | Direct, in person |
| Eligible for public funding | **Yes, strongly** | No |

**The answer to give an investor is not to pick one.** It is this:

> HidraReview is the commercial product. Every business that joins gets a
> `.hidra` address and becomes a node on the network. The local reputation
> business pays the bills and, at the same time, populates the network with real
> content and real users — the problem that kills every new decentralised
> network. The network is not a second product: it is the first one's moat.

This narrative only works if it is true in the product. See
[document 08 — Roadmap](08-roadmap.md) for the work needed to make it true.

## 3. How the privacy sector makes money (evidence)

Four models, all real, with numbers:

| Company | Model | Revenue | Users |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brave** | Tracking-free advertising + search revenue share; keeps 30% of user ads and 15% of publisher ads | **>$100M ARR** (2025–26), up from $52M in 2024 | **100M MAU** (Sep 2025) |
| **Proton** | Freemium subscription on useful services (mail, VPN, drive) | **$97.5M ARR** (2024), up from $70M in 2022 | — |
| **Mullvad** | Flat price, no tiers, no advertising, no account | — | — |
| **Tor Project** | Non-profit: donations and grants | — | 2–2.5M/day |

**The three lessons this gives HidraNet:**

1. **The Brave model is out of reach.** It requires ~100M users to reach $100M.
   With zero users, it is not a plan — it is a destination.
2. **The Proton model is the viable path.** Freemium on *useful services*, not on
   the privacy layer itself. Nobody pays for privacy; they pay for mail, storage,
   domains — and choose the private provider. HidraNet has HidraMail, `.hidra`
   and HidraReview: those are the billable services.
3. **The Tor Project model is what funds the start.** Grants for the protocol
   layer, open source. It is not an alternative to the business: it is what
   sustains it before there is revenue.

## 4. Market risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free competitors (Tor/Brave/Mullvad) make the network unmonetisable | **High** | High | Revenue comes from Market B; the network is differentiation, not a revenue line |
| Chat Control passes in a form covering HidraChat/HidraMail | Medium | **High** | See doc 10; content-blind architecture, entity outside scope |
| Reputational association with illicit markets | Medium | **High** | Positioned in legitimate local commerce (HidraReview) from day one |
| Banks / payment processors refusing anonymity services | **High** | High | Bill HidraReview as reputation SaaS, not as an anonymity service |
| Tor market stable, not growing | Confirmed | Medium | Do not depend on organic growth in demand for anonymity |
| Two products, two GTMs, two founders | **High** | High | Explicit focus: HidraReview commercial, network open source funded by grants |
