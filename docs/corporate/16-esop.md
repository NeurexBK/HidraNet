# Stock Option Policy (ESOP) — HidraNet

**Version 1.0 · September 2026**

> ⚠️ **A TEMPLATE FOR LEGAL AND TAX REVIEW.** The tax treatment of option plans
> in Portugal has significant particularities (see section 8) and must be
> confirmed with a lawyer and an accountant **before** the first grant. See also
> the Lda/SA question in document 02, section 6.

---

## 1. Purpose

The plan exists so that whoever builds HidraNet shares in the value they help
create. In an early-stage company that cannot compete on salary with established
employers, it is the instrument that allows it to attract and retain people.

**Pool size: 10% of fully diluted capital** (100,000 units), created before the
Seed round.

## 3. Reference grant table

Percentages of fully diluted capital. **Reference values**, adjustable according
to the company's stage — whoever joins earlier takes more risk and receives more.

| Role | First 10 | Employee 11–30 | After 30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Senior engineer / Tech lead | 0.50 – 1.50% | 0.25 – 0.75% | 0.10 – 0.40% |
| Engineer | 0.25 – 0.75% | 0.10 – 0.40% | 0.05 – 0.20% |
| Junior engineer | 0.10 – 0.30% | 0.05 – 0.15% | 0.02 – 0.08% |
| Sales | 0.20 – 0.60% | 0.10 – 0.30% | 0.05 – 0.15% |
| Design / Product | 0.25 – 0.75% | 0.10 – 0.40% | 0.05 – 0.20% |
| C-level | 1.00 – 3.00% | 0.50 – 1.50% | 0.25 – 0.75% |
| Operations / Admin | 0.10 – 0.30% | 0.05 – 0.15% | 0.02 – 0.08% |

**Fairness principle:** people with equivalent roles and seniority receive
equivalent grants. Differences have to be justifiable on objective criteria — not
on negotiating ability. A plan perceived as arbitrary demotivates more than no
plan at all.

## 4. Vesting

### 4.1 Standard terms

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Total period | **4 years** |
| Cliff | **12 months** — nothing vests before |
| After the cliff | 25% vests at once |
| Thereafter | Monthly, 1/48 of the total per month |

**Example.** A grant of 4,800 options:

| Point in time | Vested | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|
| Months 1–11 | 0 | 0 |
| Month 12 (cliff) | 1,200 | 1,200 (25%) |
| Months 13–48 | 100/month | — |
| Month 48 | — | 4,800 (100%) |

### 4.2 Acceleration

| Event | Acceleration |
|---|---|
| Acquisition of the company | **50%** of unvested options (single trigger) |
| Acquisition + termination without cause within 12 months | **100%** (double trigger) |
| Death or permanent incapacity | **100%** |

---

## 5. Exercise

### 5.1 Price

The exercise price is the **fair market value at the grant date**, determined by
reference to the last investment round or, failing that, by independent
valuation.

Whoever joins early benefits from a low exercise price. That is the reward for
taking the risk of joining early.

### 5.2 Deadlines

| Situation | Time to exercise |
|---|---|
| During the relationship | From vesting, up to 10 years from the grant |
| Voluntary departure | **12 months** after it ends |
| Termination without cause | **12 months** |
| Termination for cause | **Immediate forfeiture**, including vested options |
| Retirement, death, incapacity | 24 months (heirs, where applicable) |

> **A note on the 12 months.** The industry's historical standard is 90 days,
> which in practice forces whoever leaves to buy the options immediately or lose
> them — an unfair choice for someone who contributed. We adopt 12 months
> deliberately, because someone who worked on the project for years deserves time
> to decide.

## 6. Advisors

A separate scale, with shorter periods:

| Involvement | Grant | Vesting |
|---|---|---|
| Occasional (a few hours per quarter) | 0.05 – 0.10% | 2 years, 3-month cliff |
| Regular (monthly) | 0.10 – 0.25% | 2 years, 3-month cliff |
| Intensive (weekly, operational involvement) | 0.25 – 0.50% | 2 years, 3-month cliff |

Formalised by an advisory agreement with concrete objectives. **An advisor who
does not contribute does not vest** — the termination-for-inactivity clause
should be in the agreement.

## 7. Governance of the plan

### 7.1 Who decides

| Decision | Authority |
|---|---|
| Individual grants up to 0.5% | Management/Board |
| Grants above 0.5% | Management + investors with veto rights |
| Increasing the pool | Shareholders' meeting |
| Amending the rules | Shareholders' meeting |

### 7.2 Transparency

Each beneficiary receives, in writing:

1. A **grant agreement** with the number of options, exercise price, vesting
   schedule and exercise deadlines
2. A **copy of these rules**
3. A **summary cap table** — how many options over what total, so that the
   percentage means something
4. An **annual update** on vesting status

> **Principle:** an option whose holder does not know what it represents
> motivates nobody. "0.5% of the company" only means something if the person
> knows what the total is, what the exercise price is and what happens if they
> leave. We explain that at the outset, in writing, even if it dulls the
> announcement.

## 8. ⚠️ Tax — to be analysed before the first grant

**This section flags the problem; it does not solve it. It requires tax advice.**

In Portugal, the tax treatment of option plans has significant particularities:

| Moment | Question to confirm |
|---|---|
| Grant | Is there a taxable event? Generally not, but it depends on the structure |
| Vesting | Is there taxation on vesting? |
| Exercise | Generally, the difference between market value and exercise price is employment income (category A) |
| Sale | Capital gain (category G) |

**The concrete risk to avoid:** that the employee is taxed at exercise on a gain
**they have not yet realised in cash**, because the holdings are illiquid. It is
a real problem, which has already left people with tax to pay on shares they
could not sell.

**Regimes to analyse with the accountant:**
- The regime applicable to startups certified by Startup Portugal, which provides
  specific conditions for option plans
- The possibility of deferring taxation to the point of sale

**Action:** confirm the treatment **before** the first grant. Fixing it
afterwards is expensive and affects people who trusted the plan.

## 9. Frequently asked questions (for employees)

**Are options shares?**
No. They are the right to buy holdings at a fixed price, once vested. They only
become holdings when you exercise and pay.

**What are they worth?**
Today, the market value is whatever the last round implies. In an early-stage
company, **they may end up worth nothing.** That is not a formal caveat: most
startups never reach a liquidity event. Treat them as a share in the success, not
as pay.

**What if the company is sold?**
You receive the value corresponding to your vested options, less the exercise
price, with the acceleration in section 4.2.

**What if I leave?**
You keep what has vested and have 12 months to exercise. Anything unvested is
forfeited.

**Can I sell before the company is sold?**
Generally no. The holdings are illiquid and subject to pre-emption rights. Future
rounds may bring an opportunity for a partial sale (a secondary), but it is not
guaranteed.

**Can I negotiate the grant?**
Yes, within the table in section 3. What we do not do is grant outside the table
on negotiating ability — that would break the fairness that makes the plan
credible.
